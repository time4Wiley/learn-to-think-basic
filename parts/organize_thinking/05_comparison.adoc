= Comparison

[.chapter-intro]

Welcome to Chapter 5, where we'll learn about comparison - a powerful thinking skill that combines what we learned about similarities and differences. In this chapter, you'll discover how looking at both what makes things alike and different helps us understand them better.

[TTS]

1. Combine our knowledge of similarities and differences

== Understanding Comparison

[TTS]
....
Remember how we looked at similarities in Chapter 2 and differences in Chapter 3? Now we'll put these skills together to get a complete picture of how things relate to each other.
....

[quote]
____
Yes, it's possible to think about how two things are different and how they are the same at the same time. This gives us a fuller understanding of both things we're comparing.
____

== Using SCUMPS for Comparison

[TTS]
....
Just as we used SCUMPS in earlier chapters, we can use it here to help us make thorough comparisons. Let's look at each aspect:
Size - How do their sizes compare?
Color - What colors do they share or not share?
Use - How are their uses similar or different?
Material - What materials do they share or not share?
Parts - Which parts are similar or different?
Shape - How do their shapes compare?
....

== Example Analysis

[TTS]
....
Let's look at a detailed comparison between sharks and cats. This example shows how we can organize our thoughts about both similarities and differences.
....

.Comparing Sharks and Cats
[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
|Sharks Only |Both Have |Cats Only

|Can swim
|Eat meat
|Can meow

|Have no legs
|Have blood
|Can climb trees

|Have gills
|Have tails
|Kept as pets
|===

[TIP]
====
When comparing things, look at:
* What only the first thing has
* What they both share
* What only the second thing has
====

== Extension Activities

[TTS]
....
Here are some additional activities to help you master comparison skills.
....

1. Create a "Comparison Journal"
* Pick two things to compare each day
* Use the three-column format
* Include drawings or pictures
* Write about why the differences and similarities matter

2. Play "Compare and Contrast"
* Choose two objects
* Take turns naming similarities
* Take turns naming differences
* The person who finds the most wins!

[TIP]
====
The better you become at comparison, the easier it will be to:
* Make good decisions
